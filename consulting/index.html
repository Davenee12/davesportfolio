What we do

Three practices. One outcome.

Institutions that act on their data.

Most higher-ed analytics work fails at the seams — the SQL doesn't match the dashboard, the dashboard doesn't match the meeting, the meeting doesn't change anything. We work end-to-end so the seams disappear.

Dashboards

Executive-ready BI

Power BI dashboards that survive contact with cabinet meetings. Built from your real data warehouse, not slide decks. Versioned, documented, and handed off cleanly.

  • Enrollment, retention & FTIC cohort dashboards
  • IPEDS-aligned reporting surfaces
  • People analytics & HR dashboards
  • Row-level security & FERPA-safe design
SQL & data

Data engineering

The pipeline behind the dashboard. PostgreSQL models, semantic layers, and documented query libraries your IR team can actually maintain after we leave.

  • Cohort & longitudinal query frameworks
  • Reusable view libraries in Git; ETL & Power Query refactoring

  • Automation of recurring compliance reports

How we work

Four phases, shaped around your academic calendar.

Higher ed runs on terms, not sprints.

Our process respects census dates, board cycles, and reporting deadlines — and produces artifacts your team owns at the end.

01

Discover

Stakeholder interviews, data inventory, and a clear scoping doc. We map what exists, what's broken, and what the board actually wants to see.

02

Model

SQL models in Git, semantic layer documented, FERPA review baked in. Every metric defined once, used everywhere.

03

Build

Dashboards, tools, or both — iterated weekly with your IR team in the loop. No black-box deliverables.

04

Hand off

Documentation, training, and a 30-day support runway. Your team owns it. We're a phone call away when you need us.

Why higher ed only

A consultancy that already knows your data model.

Most analytics consultancies treat higher education as a vertical to tack on. We do the opposite. Every engagement we take is for a college, university, or higher-ed adjacent organization — because the difference between a generic BI shop and one that already knows what FTIC, FFTE, and IPEDS Part E mean is measured in months of wasted ramp-up.

The practice is led by Davenee James — Enterprise Analytics Specialist at the University of Central Florida and former IR analyst at Southwestern Adventist University, where the Argos-to-Power BI migration, the Colleague ODS reporting layer, and five annual IPEDS surveys were built from the inside.
